# Independent Research Project Ideas
## Learning Rules for Life-Like Emergent Behavior — Ferienakademie 2026

These are starting points rather than fixed assignments. Each group should turn one idea into a focused research question, build a working simulation or analysis, choose useful observables or evaluation criteria, and present the main results together with limitations.

## 1. Swarm systems | Extend one of the SwarmRules notebooks [1–3]

Build on Notebook 1 or 2 by changing the task, environment, geometry, communication constraints, number of objects, robustness requirements, or search method. Possible directions include harder routing problems, multiple cargoes, adaptation to perturbations, minimal communication, automatic parameter search, or novelty search.

## 2. Collective transport | Piano-movers puzzle [4]

Starting from the cooperative-transport model of Dreyer *et al.*, implement a simplified version of the T-shaped-load puzzle and compare different local transport rules. A particularly interesting goal is to simplify the published model and determine how close it can be brought to the much simpler SwarmRules framework while retaining successful collective problem solving.

## 3. Cellular automata | Search for life-like rules [5–6]

Explore discrete cellular automata, continuous cellular automata such as Lenia, or a small custom rule family and search for persistent, mobile, self-repairing, or otherwise interesting structures. Possible approaches include manual exploration, objective-based optimization, evolutionary search, or novelty/diversity search.

## 4. Particle life | Ventrella-style Clusters [7–9]

Implement a multi-species particle system with simple, possibly asymmetric attraction and repulsion rules and investigate which interaction matrices generate stable clusters, oscillators, moving structures, or other life-like dynamics. Possible extensions include automated search, comparison with related particle-life models, or mapping a small phase diagram.

## 5. Biological modeling | Collective behavior in *C. elegans* [10–12]

Develop your own model for collective behavior observed in *C. elegans* and test whether it can reproduce some of the reported aggregation, pattern formation, or swarming dynamics. Possible ingredients include density-dependent motility, oxygen sensing/aerotaxis, and environmental fields, but the central challenge is to decide which mechanisms are actually necessary.
